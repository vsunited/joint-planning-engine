# Participant script

Read this aloud. Do not paraphrase, and use the same words for both runs — the
briefing is part of the experimental condition.

## Briefing

> Thank you for helping with this. I am measuring how long it takes to produce
> an initial warning order, and how complete the result is, using two different
> methods.
>
> You will do this twice, on different days, with a different scenario each
> time. Once using whatever you normally use — Word, a unit template, whatever
> your actual workflow is. Once using a planning application I have built.
>
> I am measuring the methods, not you. There is no score attached to you, and
> nothing here goes into any record. If one of these turns out to be slower or
> more awkward, that is a result I need — a finding that the tool does not help
> is as useful to me as one that says it does, and considerably more useful
> than a flattering number I cannot trust.
>
> You will get a directive from higher headquarters on screen. Produce the
> initial WARNORD you would send to subordinate commands.
>
> There are three buttons on the bar at the bottom. Press **Packet opened**
> when you start reading. Press **Drafting started** when you write your first
> real words. Press **WARNORD complete** when you would be content to release
> it. That last one is your call — when *you* judge it releasable, not when you
> think I want you to stop.
>
> While you work I cannot help you. If you ask me a doctrinal question I will
> tell you to use your own judgement, and that is not me being difficult — if I
> answer, I have changed the thing I am measuring.
>
> The timing is stored on this machine only. Nothing you type is recorded — the
> log stores which field you edited and how long the text ended up, never its
> content. Your name is not recorded anywhere; you are a code.
>
> You can stop at any point, and you can ask me afterwards to delete your data.
> Do you have any questions before we start?

## Tool-arm addition

> This application walks the joint planning process. You have not used it
> before, and I am not going to train you — how usable it is without training
> is part of what I am measuring. Explore it however you like.

## During the run

- Do not answer doctrinal questions. "Use your own judgement."
- Do not touch the keyboard or the mouse.
- Do not react to the clock or to what they are writing.
- Write down anything notable: hesitations, misreadings, complaints, anything
  they say aloud. Those observations are often worth more than the timing.

## Afterwards

Ask, and record verbatim:

1. What slowed you down most?
2. Was anything missing that you would normally include?
3. *(Tool arm)* What did the tool do that you would not have done yourself?
4. *(Tool arm)* Where did it get in your way?

Then confirm again that they may withdraw their data, and tell them when you
expect to have results.
